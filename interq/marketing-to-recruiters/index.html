How to market an interview app to recruiters and HR teams

InterQ has a two-sided go-to-market: recruiters must choose to use it, and candidates must be willing to download it. Here is how we would position it, run first pilots and pick channels.

ShoutEx Team · Updated October 2026 · Page 4 of 5
InterQ / Marketing to recruiters

How should InterQ be positioned?

Position InterQ on better interviews and a better candidate experience, not on assessment. Recruiters do not want another test to administer. They want interviews that start naturally, reveal more about the candidate and leave a good impression of the employer. The video's line, "don't just interview, InterQ", already states that position well.

The jobs-to-be-done lens helps keep the message honest. Clayton Christensen and colleagues explain it in Harvard Business Review's Know Your Customers' "Jobs to Be Done". For a recruiter, the job is closer to "help me have a real conversation with this person in the first five minutes" than "test this candidate".

How do you get candidates to download the app?

Candidates download InterQ because the recruiter asks them to, so the recruiter's invitation is the real acquisition channel. Give recruiters a short invitation template that explains what the test is, how long it takes and that it is a conversation starter, not a pass or fail. The app store listing must then confirm the same message in the first screenshot.

Candidates are clear about what makes an interview process stand out. LinkedIn's Talent Blog collected what candidates say makes an interview process stand out, and transparency about each step is a recurring theme. Use it to shape the invitation wording.

Hint

Put the expected completion time in the invitation email, the store listing and the app's first screen. A visible time estimate is one of the simplest ways to raise completion.

Who should InterQ sell to first?

Start with recruitment agencies and small in-house talent teams. They decide quickly, interview often and can see results within a few weeks. Large enterprises bring longer security reviews, procurement and legal sign-off, which can stall an early product for months.

Our guide on how companies purchase mobile apps covers what changes when an app moves from an individual user's choice to a company purchase, including approvals, invoices and the questions procurement asks.

Founder rule

Pilot with recruiters who interview every week.

A pilot needs volume to teach you anything. One busy agency recruiter will tell you more in two weeks than a corporate team that hires twice a quarter.

Which channels reach recruiters?

Recruiters are reachable in the places they already spend time: LinkedIn, recruiter communities and HR events. In our experience, founder-led posts that show a real response sheet and a real interview story outperform paid ads in the first year, because the product is easiest to understand when people see the output.

Founders building B2B products share honest numbers on sales cycles, pricing and early traction in communities such as r/SaaS and Indie Hackers. Use them to test your plan against other founders' experience, not to find recruiters.

How should an interview app be priced?

Charge the hiring side, never the candidate. Simple options are a per-recruiter monthly plan or a pack of candidate invitations. Per-recruiter pricing is easy to understand and grows as teams adopt it; invitation packs suit agencies with uneven hiring volume. Test one model with your first customers rather than offering both.

Important

Hiring volume follows the economy and the calendar. Expect slower months and plan pilots around the busy hiring periods your target customers describe.
