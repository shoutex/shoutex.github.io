Designing the candidate test and the response sheet

InterQ's value sits in two pieces of design: a short test candidates are happy to finish, and a response sheet recruiters actually bring into the interview. Here is how we would approach both.

ShoutEx Team · Updated October 2026 · Page 2 of 5
InterQ / The test and response sheet

What should an interview icebreaker test ask?

Ask questions with no single right answer, where the reasoning is the interesting part. InterQ's test covers logic, game theory and critical thinking. Short scenarios work well: a negotiation, a shared resource, a choice between cooperating and acting alone. The candidate's answer and their short explanation give the recruiter something real to discuss.

Game theory is a good source because its classic situations are simple to describe and rich to talk about. The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy's entry on game theory covers the standard scenarios, such as the prisoner's dilemma, and why reasonable people choose differently in them.

Question typeExample formatWhat it opens up in the interview
LogicA short puzzle with a clear structureHow the candidate breaks down a problem
Game theoryA cooperate or compete scenarioHow they think about other people's choices and trust
Critical thinkingEvaluate a claim with incomplete informationHow they handle uncertainty and ask for evidence

How long should the test be?

Keep it short enough to finish on a phone in one sitting. The video calls it a short test, and that matters: a candidate who has already filled in an application will drop off if the next step feels like an exam. Plan for a handful of questions and measure completion time from the first release.

Hint

Time yourself and three friends on the full test on a phone, standing up. If anyone takes longer than they expected, cut a question.

What should the response sheet show the recruiter?

The response sheet should show each question, the candidate's choice, their explanation in their own words and one suggested follow-up question for the interview. It should not show a total score, a ranking or a personality label. The goal stated in the video is open discussion and thought exchange, and a score would end the discussion before it starts.

  • One page, readable on a phone. Recruiters often glance at the sheet minutes before the interview.
  • Follow-up prompts. A suggested question per answer, such as "What would change your choice here?", makes the sheet useful to a busy interviewer.
  • Clear purpose. A line at the top saying the sheet is a conversation starter, not an assessment result.

How do you keep the test fair to every candidate?

Treat fairness as a product requirement. In the United States, the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission's guidance on employment tests and selection procedures explains that tests used to make hiring decisions can create legal risk if they screen out protected groups without being job-related. Positioning InterQ as a conversation tool, not a selection test, is the right product decision and the right legal posture.

Accessibility is part of fairness. Build the test to the W3C's Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2: readable text sizes, screen reader support and no time pressure that disadvantages candidates who need more time.

Important

This page is general product guidance, not legal advice. Have an employment lawyer review how buyers are told to use the response sheet before you sell to employers in a new country or province.

What should be in version one?

Version one needs four things: the candidate app, the test, the response sheet and reliable email delivery to the recruiter. Leave out recruiter dashboards, integrations with applicant tracking systems, custom question banks and analytics until recruiters ask for them by name. Our guide on how to choose MVP features has a simple scoring method for that list.

HR practitioners are blunt about what tools they actually use. Reading threads in r/humanresources before planning version two will save you from building features nobody turns on.

Founder rule

Ship the sheet before the dashboard.

Recruiters judge InterQ by the response sheet in their inbox. Make that one page excellent before building any screen for them to log into.
