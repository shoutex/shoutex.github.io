Building InterQ: app stores, email delivery and privacy

InterQ looks simple from the outside: download, answer, email. Underneath, four engineering decisions decide whether it works: how it ships through the app stores, how the test is delivered, how the sheet reaches the recruiter and how candidate data is protected.

ShoutEx Team · Updated October 2026 · Page 3 of 5
InterQ / Building the app

Why ship InterQ as an app store app?

InterQ asks the candidate to download the app, which gives it a trusted install path, a place in the candidate's phone and a store listing that doubles as marketing. The cost is review. Apple's App Store Review Guidelines set rules on privacy disclosures, account deletion, data use and minimum functionality, and a hiring app collects personal data from the first screen.

Plan the privacy labels, the data deletion flow and the review notes before the first submission. Rejections usually come from missing disclosures, not from the core idea.

Hint

Write the App Store privacy label and the privacy policy in the same afternoon, from one shared list of every data field the app collects. Mismatches between the two are a common reason for review delays.

How should the test be delivered?

Keep the questions on the server and download them when the candidate starts. That lets you change wording, add languages or fix a confusing question without shipping a new app version and waiting for review. Save each answer on the phone as the candidate goes, so a dropped connection or an incoming call never costs them their progress.

Avoid heavy anti-cheating features. InterQ's answers are talking points, not a pass mark, so locking the phone or tracking the camera adds friction and privacy risk for no real gain.

How do you make sure the response sheet reaches the recruiter?

Email is the product's delivery truck, so treat deliverability as core engineering work. Send from your own domain through a transactional email provider, and set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC records before launch. Google's email sender guidelines list the authentication and formatting rules Gmail applies, and corporate mail filters are often stricter.

  • Send a web link and a PDF. The link always works on a phone; the PDF suits recruiters who forward sheets to hiring managers.
  • Confirm delivery to the candidate. A short "your sheet was sent" message reduces anxious follow-up emails.
  • Log bounces. A wrong recruiter address is the most common failure, and only the logs will show it.

What does candidate privacy require?

Candidates share how they think, which is personal information, with a company they do not work for yet. Collect only what the response sheet needs, explain clearly who will see it, and delete it on a set schedule. In Canada, the Office of the Privacy Commissioner summarizes PIPEDA's requirements in brief, including consent, limiting collection and safeguarding data.

For the build itself, the OWASP Mobile Top 10 is a useful checklist: secure local storage, encrypted traffic, no secrets in the app code and proper authentication for anything a recruiter can open.

Important

Privacy law differs between provinces, states and countries. Confirm your consent wording and data retention with a privacy lawyer before selling to employers outside your home market.

Which tech stack fits an app like InterQ?

A cross-platform framework such as React Native or Flutter is a sensible default here. The app is mostly forms, text and simple navigation, with no heavy camera or hardware use, so one codebase for iOS and Android saves real money. A small server handles questions, sheet generation and email.

Our guide on choosing an MVP tech stack walks through the trade-offs in more detail, including when native development is worth the extra cost.

Founder rule

Never let the candidate lose an answer.

Save every answer on the phone the moment it is given. One lost test costs you a candidate's trust and a recruiter's sheet at the same time.
