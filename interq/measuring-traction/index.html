How to measure traction for an interview app like InterQ

Downloads say little about a hiring app. Whether candidates finish the test, whether recruiters open the sheet and whether they keep inviting candidates tell you far more. Here are the numbers to watch.

ShoutEx Team · Updated October 2026 · Page 5 of 5
InterQ / Measuring traction

Which metrics matter for InterQ?

Measure the full chain from invitation to interview. The core metric is the share of invited candidates whose response sheet reaches the recruiter before the interview. Around it, track test completion, time to complete, sheet open rate and how many recruiters invite candidates again the following month. Revenue follows these numbers.

MetricWhat it tells youWhere it comes from
Invitation to completed testWhether candidates trust and finish the testApp analytics
Median completion timeWhether the test is short enoughTimestamps in the app
Sheet open rateWhether recruiters actually look at the outputEmail and link tracking
Recruiters active again next monthWhether InterQ became part of their routineYour database
Interviewer feedbackWhether the sheet improved the conversationA one-question survey after the interview

How do you know the app is stable enough?

A crash in the middle of a test feels personal to a candidate who is already nervous about an interview. On Android, Google Play's Android vitals reports crash rates and app-not-responding errors. Track them weekly alongside failed email deliveries from your own logs.

Hint

Add a short line at the end of the test asking how it felt. Candidate comments in the first month will tell you more than any dashboard.

When does an interview app have product-market fit?

You are close to product-market fit when recruiters invite candidates without reminders, ask to add colleagues and bring InterQ with them when they change jobs. Marc Andreessen's essay on why product/market fit is the only thing that matters, kept on a Stanford course site, describes the point where the market starts pulling the product forward.

Our FAQ on how to know if you have product-market fit gives a practical checklist for B2B products. For InterQ we would add one test: would recruiters complain if the response sheet stopped arriving before their interviews?

Founder rule

Count repeat recruiters, not downloads.

Candidates download the app because they are asked to. The number that shows real demand is how many recruiters keep asking.

Should you ask recruiters how likely they are to recommend InterQ?

Yes, but ask at the right moment. Fred Reichheld's Harvard Business Review article The One Number You Need to Grow introduced the approach behind Net Promoter Score. Ask recruiters after their fifth interview using InterQ, and ask candidates separately after the test, because the two groups judge the product on different things.

Pair the score with one open question: "What did you do before InterQ?" The answers show your real competitor, which is usually an unstructured first ten minutes rather than another app.

Where can you compare notes with other founders?

Founder communities such as r/startups are useful for comparing early retention and pilot conversion with other B2B products. Treat what you read there as anecdote, not benchmark, and weigh your own recruiter interviews more heavily.

Key point

Repeat use by recruiters predicts revenue. If it is flat, improve the response sheet before you spend more on marketing.
