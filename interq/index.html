InterQ: a use case for a vertical mobile app in hiring

InterQ is a mobile app that helps candidates and recruiters break the ice in a job interview. This guide uses it to show founders and product owners how to plan, build and market a mobile app for one specific vertical.

ShoutEx Team · Updated October 2026 · Page 1 of 5
InterQ use case

What is InterQ?

InterQ is a mobile app that gives a job interview a better starting point. Before the interview, the candidate downloads the app and completes a short test based on logic, game theory and critical thinking. The app turns the answers into a response sheet and emails it to the recruiter. The interview then opens with a conversation about how the candidate thinks, instead of a recital of their resume.

The promise is in the tagline: don't just interview, InterQ. The product does not try to score or rank people. It gives both sides something specific to talk about, so they can build rapport faster.

Why use InterQ as a vertical app use case?

InterQ is a clear example of vertical software: it serves one workflow, in one industry, for two very specific users. That makes it a useful model for any founder building a focused mobile app instead of a general tool. The same questions come up in every vertical: who uses it, who pays, what the first version must do and how to reach buyers.

Our guide to marketing vertical software apps explains why narrow products often win against suites. They speak the buyer's language, fit one workflow closely and are easier to explain in a single sentence. InterQ's single sentence is "an app that helps break the ice during the interview".

Who uses InterQ and who pays for it?

InterQ has two users with different reasons to care. The candidate uses the app once, before an interview. The recruiter or hiring manager receives the response sheet and uses it in many interviews. The person who pays is usually on the hiring side: an in-house talent team, an HR manager or a recruitment agency.

PersonWhat they wantWhat would stop them
CandidateA fair chance to show how they think, not only what is on their resumeA long test, a confusing app or worry about how answers are used
RecruiterA natural way to open the interview and learn something newAnother tool to manage, or a sheet that adds nothing
Hiring managerBetter conversations and a clearer read on how a candidate reasonsAnything that looks like an unvalidated scoring test
HR or talent lead (buyer)A better candidate experience and consistent interviewsPrivacy, legal or brand risk

Candidate experience is a real buying reason for talent teams. LinkedIn's Talent Blog has a practical overview of what candidate experience is and why it matters to employers competing for people.

Hint

Write two value propositions, one for the candidate and one for the buyer, before you design a single screen. If they conflict, the buyer's version usually decides the roadmap.

Where does InterQ fit in the hiring process?

InterQ sits between scheduling the interview and the first conversation. It does not replace the interview or the applicant tracking system. It adds a short, structured step that gives every candidate the same starting point, which is also what research on fair interviews recommends.

Harvard professor Iris Bohnet's article on how to take the bias out of interviews argues for structure: the same questions, in the same order, judged the same way. A shared starting point like the response sheet supports that structure, as long as the sheet guides the conversation rather than replacing judgement.

Founder rule

Sell the conversation, not a score.

A tool that ranks candidates invites legal, privacy and fairness questions from every buyer. A tool that improves the first ten minutes of an interview is easier to approve, easier to love and harder to replace.

Where do recruiters and HR teams talk about tools?

Spend time where recruiters already discuss their work before you write marketing copy. The r/recruiting community on Reddit shows the daily frustrations of agency and in-house recruiters in their own words. The Society for Human Resource Management (SHRM) publishes research, templates and events that shape how HR leaders think about hiring.
