Pagezii MVP roadmap: measuring brands inside AI answers

Pagezii studies how brands are discovered, recommended and measured across ChatGPT, Google AI, Gemini and other AI search tools. This is a proposed technical roadmap for turning that research into a first product, written for a founder and a CTO.

ShoutEx Team · Updated October 2026 · Page 1 of 4
Pagezii MVP roadmap

What does Pagezii need to measure?

Pagezii's research covers three layers: which prompts drive commercial discovery, how often a brand is mentioned, recommended or cited in AI answers, and how that discovery connects to visits and conversions. An MVP has to turn each layer into a number that can be collected the same way every week and compared over time.

The research base is real and growing. The GEO: Generative Engine Optimization paper introduced visibility metrics for generative engines, and newer work proposes a measurement framework for citations across AI search platforms. Pagezii's own note on how LLMs decide which brands to recommend summarizes why this is a different problem from ranking pages. Our guide on what AI search visibility is covers the marketing side.

What does the system architecture look like?

The system is a batch pipeline, not a real-time product. A prompt library feeds collectors that query each AI engine on a schedule, every raw answer is stored unchanged, and parsers turn answers into structured mentions and citations. Metrics and attribution sit on top. Keeping raw answers is the most important design decision, because the parsers will improve and you will want to re-run them on old data.

LayerSuggested stackWhy
OrchestrationPython workers on a job queue, scheduled by cronSimple, cheap, easy to rate-limit per engine
StoragePostgreSQL with JSONB for raw answersOne database for raw data, parsed rows and metrics
Embeddingspgvector in the same databasePrompt clustering without a separate vector store
ParsingRules first, then an LLM judge with structured outputRules are cheap and testable; the judge handles nuance
ReportingSQL views feeding a lightweight dashboardResearchers can query directly
CTO rule

Store the raw answer forever.

Every metric in this roadmap is a parse of the raw text. If you only store the parsed result, every improvement to the parser starts from zero data.

What are the roadmap phases?

The roadmap has four phases, each with an exit test instead of a date. A phase ends when its numbers are stable and trusted enough to build on, not when a sprint finishes.

PhaseGoalDeliverableExit test
1. Visibility collectorMeasure mentions and citationsCollectors for 3 engines, raw store, mention and citation parsersWeek-over-week mention rate moves less than the sampling noise on an unchanged prompt set
2. Prompt intelligenceKnow which prompts matterPrompt library, intent taxonomy, clusteringEvery tracked prompt belongs to a labelled cluster that a marketer recognizes
3. AttributionConnect AI discovery to visitsAI referral channel, crawler log parsing, joined reportsAI-referred sessions and conversions reported per engine, with known gaps written down
4. Conversational adsStudy ads inside AI answersAd exposure logging and lift analysis as a research moduleA repeatable method, documented in a research note

Conversational advertising is now live in at least one major assistant: OpenAI announced it was testing ads in ChatGPT. That makes Phase 4 a research module worth planning for, but not one to build first, because ads are measured best on top of organic visibility data.

Hint

Write each phase's exit test into the repository README before any code. It stops scope creep and gives the founder and CTO the same definition of done.
