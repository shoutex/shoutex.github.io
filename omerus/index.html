Omerus: an iPad calculator built around gestures

Omerus is a calculator ShoutEx built for the iPad. It uses the whole screen to show, store and reuse numbers, and it supports basic math through hand gestures. Here is what it does and what a product owner can learn from it.

ShoutEx Team · Updated October 2026 · One-page use case
Omerus use case

What is Omerus?

Omerus is an iPad calculator that performs basic operations through natural hand gestures. Every calculation is kept as a line, in the order it was made, and any number already on screen can be reused with a tap. Lines can be edited, deleted and given labels, worksheets keep projects apart, and a finished worksheet can be emailed as a PDF. ShoutEx launched it worldwide on the App Store.

"We developed Omerus to address a basic need in the market," said Zaki Usman, CEO of ShoutEx: a simple, capable calculator that uses the entire iPad screen.

How does the Omerus screen work?

The design treats a calculation as a record, not a disappearing result. That one decision drives the rest of the feature set: lines need order, so they are chronological; records need context, so lines take labels; and records get reused, so earlier numbers are tappable.

  • Gestures. Basic operations are entered by hand gestures instead of only a keypad.
  • Tap to reuse. Any number shown earlier can be dropped into the current line.
  • Editable history. Lines stay in order and can be edited or deleted.
  • Labels and worksheets. Each line can carry a label, and each project gets its own worksheet.
  • PDF by email. A worksheet can be shared with colleagues as a PDF.

Getting there meant leaving things out. Usman described the work as finding the right balance between UI elements. Our guide on choosing MVP features covers how to make those cuts on purpose.

What should a product owner know about building it?

Three areas carry most of the engineering risk in an app like this.

  • Gesture conflicts. Custom gestures compete with system gestures and with scrolling the line list. Build them on UIGestureRecognizer, keep the gesture area separate from the scrolling area, and follow Apple's Human Interface Guidelines on gestures. The WWDC session Designing Fluid Interfaces is the best primer on making gestures feel responsive.
  • Reused numbers. Decide whether a tapped number is a copy or a live reference. A reference means editing line 2 updates line 4, which is powerful but must be visible to the user. We would show the link and recalculate.
  • PDF and email. Render the worksheet with UIGraphicsPDFRenderer and attach it with MFMailComposeViewController. Fall back to the share sheet when no mail account is set up.
Important

Store worksheets locally with a clear save model. A calculator that loses a labelled worksheet loses the one thing that made it better than the default.

How should a calculator app like Omerus be marketed?

Market the worksheet, not the arithmetic. Every iPad now ships with a calculator: Apple added the Calculator app, with Math Notes, in iPadOS 18. Omerus wins when the buyer needs labelled, saved, shareable calculations for work, such as budgets, quotes and expense checks, so that is where the message and the screenshots should point.

The audience is business professionals, which puts Omerus in the productivity category. Our guides on marketing B2B productivity apps and App Store optimization cover positioning against built-in tools and choosing search terms like "budget calculator" or "calculator with history" over the crowded word "calculator".

Founder rule

Compete on the job, not the category.

Nobody switches calculators for better arithmetic. They switch because they need to keep, label and share their numbers, so lead with that.
